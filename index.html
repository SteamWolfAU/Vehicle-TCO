How it's calculated

  • Every option ends with you owning the car (balloon/residual paid), so all are compared on the same basis and the expected resale value is deducted.
  • Opportunity cost = interest each dollar would have earned from when it's spent until the end of the comparison period.
  • Each finance option has its own term. Novated leases are capped at 5 years in Australia; a lease or loan that ends early is followed by outright ownership, and one that runs past the period is paid out at the end.
  • Novated: financier claims GST on the price (capped at the car limit); employer claims GST on rentals & packaged costs. FBT is eliminated with post-tax Employee Contributions (ECM) unless the EV exemption applies. Base value drops by ⅓ from year 5.
  • Business use: running costs, interest, fees & lease rentals are deducted at your business-use %, with diminishing-value depreciation (25%) capped at the car limit. GST credits for GST-registered businesses and the luxury-car notional-loan rules for leases aren't modelled.
  • Tax uses resident rates, LITO, Medicare levy & (optional) surcharge. Estimates only — not financial or tax advice.

How it's calculated

  • Total cost of ownership = depreciation (value lost) + running costs + repairs + loan interest + opportunity cost − any business tax deductions. There's no purchase price; the car's current value is what you give up by keeping it rather than selling.
  • Opportunity cost = interest you'd earn if you sold the car today and invested the equity (value less any loan), plus interest forgone on money spent running it.
  • Cash out of pocket = what actually leaves your bank account: running costs, repairs and loan repayments.
  • Running costs rise with inflation; maintenance also rises by the ageing rate. Depreciation of the car itself isn't claimed as a tax deduction here.
